Executive summary

Electricity became AI's hardest constraint

U.S. data centers used 4.7% of the nation's electricity in 2024, and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory projects about 12% by 2030 (range 9.5–15.3%). Compute is built in months. New power plants take a median of about five years to connect to the grid, and data centers wait in their own utility queues. This summary maps where that collision is happening.

474 GW queued in Texas, about 90% of it data centers.
The 8 findings
1
Demand is outrunning supply.4.7% of U.S. power in 2024, about 12% by 2030, needing roughly 148 GW of new grid connections (LBNL).
2
A handful of metros dominate.Northern Virginia runs about 4,500 MW; Dallas–Fort Worth and Atlanta have each passed 1 GW (CBRE H1 2026).
3
Texas is the breakout market.ERCOT's large-load queue went from 63 GW (end of 2024) to about 474 GW by June 2026.
4
The queue is the real constraint.New power plants take a median of 61 months to connect. Of 2000–2020 requests, only 13% are operating; about 75% withdrew.
5
Data centers wait in their own line.Utility large-load queues vary by utility; in Virginia, Dominion is assigning connection dates out to 2031.
6
Gas is back.Gas interconnection requests jumped 86% to 253 GW in 2025, while solar, storage and wind requests fell.
7
Behind-the-meter is surging.xAI's Colossus ran 35 gas turbines (~422 MW) on an 8 MW grid connection. Now turbines are the bottleneck.
8
The next wave is moving inland.Atlanta has about 2,880 MW under construction, the most of any U.S. market; central Ohio is adding gigawatt campuses.
